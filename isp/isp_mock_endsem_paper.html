Q1 Camera Models
[5]
A pinhole camera has focal length f = 50 mm. A 3D point P = (200, 150, 1000) mm (in camera coordinates) is projected onto the image plane.
(a) Using the perspective projection equations, find the image coordinates (x′, y′).
(b) Write the full 3×4 projection matrix P = K[R | t] assuming R = I and t = 0, with principal point (cx, cy) = (320, 240) and pixel pitch 0.01 mm/pixel.
(c) What is the role of homogeneous coordinates in this formulation?
x′ = f·X/Z, y′ = f·Y/Z. The intrinsic matrix K encodes focal length and principal point. Homogeneous coordinates allow projection (a non-linear operation) to be expressed as a linear matrix multiply.
Q2 Transforms
[5]
State whether each of the following is TRUE or FALSE. Justify each answer in one or two sentences.
(a) The DFT of a real-valued image always has conjugate symmetry: F(u,v) = F*(−u,−v).
(b) A 2D DFT can be computed as two successive 1D DFTs (separability).
(c) Energy compaction in the DCT means most signal energy is concentrated in high-frequency coefficients.
(d) The Walsh-Hadamard Transform requires no multiplications, only additions and subtractions.
(e) The PCA transform basis depends on the specific dataset, unlike the DCT.
(a) T — follows from the DFT definition when f is real. (b) T — 2D DFT is separable in x and y. (c) F — energy concentrates in LOW frequencies. (d) T — WHT uses ±1 only. (e) T — KLT/PCA adapts to data covariance; DCT is data-independent.
Q3 Filtering & Enhancement
[5]
An image has intensity values {10, 10, 12, 200, 11, 9, 10} in a 1×7 neighborhood.
(a) Compute the output of a 3×1 mean filter at the center pixel (index 3, value 200).
(b) Compute the output of a 3×1 median filter at the same location.
(c) Which filter preserves edges better and why? Relate your answer to the frequency domain characteristics of the mean filter.
Mean filter kernel: [1/3, 1/3, 1/3] — acts as low-pass, blurs edges. Median is non-linear and preserves step edges while suppressing impulse noise. Sort the 3-element window for median.
Q4 Segmentation
[5]
Consider an image with two classes of pixels. Class 1 (background) has mean μ₁ = 50 and variance σ₁² = 100 with 70% of pixels. Class 2 (foreground) has mean μ₂ = 180 and variance σ₂² = 144 with 30% of pixels.
(a) Compute the within-class variance σ²_W.
(b) Compute the between-class variance σ²_B.
(c) State Otsu's theorem relating σ²_W and σ²_B to total variance σ²_T. Which quantity does Otsu's method maximise?
σ²_W = ω₁σ₁² + ω₂σ₂². σ²_B = ω₁ω₂(μ₁−μ₂)². Otsu: σ²_T = σ²_W + σ²_B is constant, so minimising σ²_W ≡ maximising σ²_B.
Q5 Image Formation
[5]
An image of size 512×512 has spatial resolution 96 dpi. Its 2D DFT shows a dominant peak at (u, v) = (±64, ±0) (after centring).
(a) Determine the spatial frequency of the pattern in cycles/cm.
(b) Determine the orientation of the sinusoidal pattern in the image domain.
(c) Bilinear interpolation is used when zooming this image by 1.5×. Describe how the output pixel at non-integer location (1.5, 2.5) is computed from the four surrounding input pixels.
Frequency in cycles/pixel = u/N. Convert to cycles/cm using dpi→dpcm (1 inch = 2.54 cm). Peak at (64,0) → horizontal stripes, orientation 0°. Bilinear: weighted average of 4 neighbours using fractional distances.
Q6 Estimation Theory
[5]
For the observation model g = Hf + η where η ~ N(0, R_n) and f ~ N(0, R_f):
(a) State the MMSE (Wiener filter) estimator for f given g.
(b) What simplification occurs in the frequency domain when H, R_f, and R_n are all circulant (diagonalised by the DFT)?
(c) What does the Wiener filter do in frequency bands where the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is very high? Very low?
MMSE: Ĥ = R_f H^T (H R_f H^T + R_n)^{-1}. In freq domain: Ŵ(u,v) = S_f H* / (|H|²S_f + S_n). High SNR → behaves like inverse filter. Low SNR → suppresses that frequency.

Q7 SVD & Rank
[10]
Consider the system Ax = b where: A = [1 2; 0.5 1; 2 4]   b = [2; 1; 4]
(a) Determine the rank of A. Justify using row reduction. [2]
(b) Does a solution x exist? Is it unique? Explain using the column space of A. [2]
(c) Compute the pseudoinverse solution x̂ = A†b using the formula A† = (A^T A)^{-1} A^T (if applicable) or justify why this form cannot be used. [4]
(d) Is Ax̂ − b = 0? What is the geometric interpretation of the residual? [2]
Row 3 = 2×Row 1, so rank(A) = 1. With rank-1 A and 2 unknowns, the system is underdetermined — infinitely many solutions exist if b is in col(A). Minimum-norm solution via pseudoinverse. Residual = projection of b onto null(A^T).
Q8 Histogram & Enhancement
[10]
An 8-level (3-bit) image has the following intensity histogram: {r₀=0: 10, r₁=1: 8, r₂=2: 15, r₃=3: 20, r₄=4: 12, r₅=5: 8, r₆=6: 5, r₇=7: 2}. Total pixels N = 80.
(a) Compute the probability mass function pᵣ(rₖ) for each level. [1]
(b) Compute the cumulative distribution function (CDF) Tₖ = Σpᵣ(rⱼ) for j ≤ k. [2]
(c) Apply histogram equalisation: sₖ = round[(L−1)·Tₖ] where L=8. Construct the mapping table and output histogram. [4]
(d) Now suppose you want to match this image's histogram to a target uniform distribution. Describe the two-step procedure (equalise both, then invert). [3]
CDF maps cumulative probability to output levels. Histogram equalisation spreads intensity values to flatten the output histogram. Histogram matching: equalise input → find target CDF → invert target CDF to get mapping.
Q9 Blur & Restoration
[10]
A motion-blurred image is modelled as g(x) = f(x) * h(x) + η(x), where h(x) is a 1D motion blur PSF of length L = 5 pixels (uniform: h = [1/5, 1/5, 1/5, 1/5, 1/5]).
(a) Sketch |H(u)| (the magnitude of the DFT of h). At which frequencies does H(u) = 0? [3]
(b) Why is naive inverse filtering (dividing by H(u)) problematic at or near these frequencies? [2]
(c) Write the Tikhonov-regularised solution in the frequency domain: F̂(u) = H*(u) G(u) / (|H(u)|² + λ) Explain the role of the regularisation parameter λ and how its choice affects the restoration quality. [3]
(d) State the Hadamard conditions for a well-posed problem. Is blind deconvolution (unknown h) well-posed? [2]
H(u) = (1/L)·sin(πuL/N)/sin(πu/N). Zeros at multiples of N/L. Inverse filter amplifies noise at zeros. λ trades off fidelity vs. smoothness. Hadamard: existence, uniqueness, stability — blind deconvolution violates uniqueness.
Q10 Transformations & Homography
[10]
Four point correspondences between two views of a planar scene are given (in pixels): p₁=(0,0)↔p₁′=(10,5)   p₂=(100,0)↔p₂′=(115,8)   p₃=(0,80)↔p₃′=(8,88)   p₄=(100,80)↔p₄′=(120,95)
(a) How many degrees of freedom does a planar homography H (3×3) have? Justify. [2]
(b) Each point correspondence contributes two equations. Set up the 8×9 linear system for estimating H using the DLT (Direct Linear Transform). [4]
(c) Why is RANSAC used in practice when estimating H from many correspondences, and what does it estimate alongside H? [2]
(d) How does a projective transformation differ from an affine transformation? Give the constraint that makes a projective transform affine. [2]
H has 8 DOF (9 entries, scale-invariant). DLT: from x′×Hx=0, each correspondence gives 2 independent equations. RANSAC: robustly estimates H and the inlier set. Affine: last row = [0, 0, 1], no perspective distortion.
Q11 Probability & Estimation
[10]
Let X and Y be jointly distributed with joint PDF: f_{X,Y}(x,y) = 2e^{−x−y},  0 ≤ y ≤ x < ∞
(a) Find the marginal PDF f_X(x). [2]
(b) Find the conditional PDF f_{Y|X}(y|x). [2]
(c) Compute E[Y|X = x]. [2]
(d) In the context of MMSE estimation, explain what E[Y|X] represents and why it minimises the mean squared error E[(Y − g(X))²] over all functions g. [4]
f_X(x) = ∫₀ˣ 2e^{−x−y} dy = 2e^{−x}(1−e^{−x}). Conditional: f_{Y|X} = f_{XY}/f_X. E[Y|X=x] = ∫y·f_{Y|X}dy. The MMSE estimator is the conditional expectation — this follows from the orthogonality principle.
Q12 Spatial Filtering
[10]
You need to locally threshold an image f(m,n) to isolate pixels significantly brighter than their surroundings, producing: g(m,n) = 1 if f(m,n) > local_mean(f(m,n)) + T, else 0 where the local mean is averaged over a 5×5 region.
(a) Write the 5×5 averaging kernel h that computes the local mean via convolution. [1]
(b) Show that computing f(m,n) − (f*h)(m,n) isolates the "local deviation" from the mean. Rewrite g(m,n) in terms of a single thresholding step on this deviation image. [3]
(c) This is a form of unsharp masking. Compare it with standard unsharp masking used in image enhancement. What is common? What differs? [3]
(d) What is the effect of increasing T on the resulting binary image g? What happens at T→0 and T→∞? [3]
h = (1/25)·ones(5,5). Deviation: d(m,n) = f(m,n) − (f*h)(m,n). g(m,n) = 1 iff d(m,n) > T. Unsharp masking: enhanced = f + α·(f − blurred). T→0: almost all pixels = 1. T→∞: no pixels = 1.

Q13 MMSE = MAP Equivalence
[10]
For the blurring model g = Hf + η, the MMSE estimator is: Ĥ_MMSE = R_f H^T (H R_f H^T + R_n)^{-1} and the MAP estimator is: Ĥ_MAP = (H^T R_n^{-1} H + R_f^{-1})^{-1} H^T R_n^{-1}
(a) State the matrix inversion lemma (Woodbury identity): (A + UCV)^{-1} = ... and identify A, U, C, V for this problem. [3]
(b) Apply the lemma to show algebraically that Ĥ_MMSE = Ĥ_MAP. [5]
(c) Without algebra, justify in words why the MMSE and MAP estimators must be identical for Gaussian signal and noise models. [2]
Woodbury: (A + UCV)^{-1} = A^{-1} − A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)^{-1}VA^{-1}. Set A = R_f^{-1}, U=H^T, C=R_n^{-1}, V=H. For Gaussians, the posterior is Gaussian: mean = mode → MMSE = MAP.
Q14 K-means & ISODATA
[10]
Consider an image with pixel intensities (1D for simplicity): {2, 3, 5, 8, 9, 15, 16, 17, 50, 52}. Apply K-means with K=3, initial cluster centres c₁=3, c₂=9, c₃=50.
(a) Perform two full iterations of K-means. Show assignment step and update step for each. [5]
(b) Does K-means converge in this case? State the convergence criterion. [1]
(c) K-means in image segmentation clusters pixels by intensity only. Is this spatially aware? Give a counter-example where K-means fails to separate two visually distinct regions with similar intensities. [2]
(d) ISODATA extends K-means with split and merge operations. Describe one condition under which ISODATA would split a cluster and one condition under which it would merge two clusters. [2]
Assign each point to nearest centre. Update centre = mean of assigned points. Convergence: centres don't change. K-means is not spatially aware — it ignores pixel location. ISODATA splits when σ within cluster > threshold; merges when inter-cluster distance < threshold.
Q15 PCA / KLT & SVD
[10]
You are given an image dataset represented as a data matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×d} (n images, d pixels each), zero-meaned.
(a) Define the covariance matrix C. How does SVD of X relate to the eigendecomposition of C? [3]
(b) If X = UΣV^T is the SVD, identify which matrix contains the principal components (eigenvectors of C) and what the singular values represent. [2]
(c) Rank-k approximation: Ẑ = U_k Σ_k V_k^T. Show that this minimises the Frobenius norm ‖X − Ẑ‖_F over all rank-k matrices. State the theorem used. [3]
(d) Compare PCA/KLT with the DCT as image compression transforms: data-dependence, optimality, and computational cost. [2]
C = X^T X / (n−1). Eigenvectors of C = columns of V (right singular vectors). Singular values squared are proportional to eigenvalues. Eckart-Young theorem: best rank-k approx uses top-k singular values. KLT is optimal but data-dependent; DCT is a fixed approx to KLT for Markov-1 processes.
Q16 Depth from Focus & PSF
[10]
A camera uses depth-from-focus to estimate scene depth. The focus measure is based on the Modified Laplacian: ML(m,n) = |2f(m,n) − f(m−1,n) − f(m+1,n)| + |2f(m,n) − f(m,n−1) − f(m,n+1)|
(a) Show that the 1D operator (2, −1, −1 centred at current pixel) approximates the second derivative. What frequency content does it emphasise? [2]
(b) Images are acquired at three focus settings (z₁ < z₂ < z₃). Describe the depth-from-focus algorithm to assign a depth estimate to each pixel. [3]
(c) The blur PSF for a defocused lens can be modelled as a disk of radius r. Write the PSF h(x,y) and compute its Fourier transform. How does the PSF change with increasing defocus? [3]
(d) Distinguish between space-invariant and space-variant blur. Under what imaging condition does space-variant blur arise? [2]
Second derivative amplifies high frequencies. Depth-from-focus: compute ML at each focus position; depth = z at max ML. Disk PSF: h = 1/(πr²) inside circle of radius r; its FT is a jinc function. Space-variant: PSF changes with position — occurs with tilt, zoom, off-axis aberrations.
Q17 HDR & Noise Models
[10]
High Dynamic Range (HDR) imaging merges multiple exposures of the same scene.
(a) A camera captures the same scene at exposure times t₁=1/500 s and t₂=1/30 s. A pixel reads 40 (out of 255) in the first image and 210 in the second. Assuming a linear camera response, estimate the true scene radiance E (in normalised units) from each exposure and comment on which is more reliable. [3]
(b) Salt-and-pepper noise corrupts an image with probability p = 0.1 (5% salt, 5% pepper). A 3×3 median filter is applied. Prove (or argue carefully) that if fewer than half the pixels in the neighbourhood are corrupted, the median filter recovers the correct value. [4]
(c) Gaussian noise and salt-and-pepper noise have different statistical properties. Which filter is optimal for each, and why? [3]
E = pixel_value / (exposure_time × gain). Near-saturation values (210/255) are less reliable due to clipping. For median proof: if <50% are noise, >50% are clean — median falls in the clean majority. Gaussian → mean/Wiener. Salt-pepper → median (non-linear, robust to outliers).
